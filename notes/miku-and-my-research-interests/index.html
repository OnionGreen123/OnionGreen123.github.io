Miku and the questions that brought me to AI

Motivation & cognition · Working note
First discussed 02 Feb 2026 · Reconstructed & edited 29 October 2026

My interest in AI is partly rooted in the culture I love. In a February 2026 statement of purpose, I wrote about Hatsune Miku and Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex as influences on how I think about relationships between people and artificial systems.

Miku is a voice-synthesis character shaped by a much wider creative community. Music, fan work, and the culture of creating and sharing helped make human–machine relationships a personal question for me. My interest in agents, cognition alignment, and emotional intelligence grew alongside that experience.

From a source of beauty to a research interest

I am interested in systems that can engage with human behavior, thought, and emotion. The cultural motivation gives that interest a personal starting point. It does not, by itself, answer the technical questions about what such systems can understand or how they should be built and evaluated.

The appeal of technology is partly the possibility of participating in the creation of something I care about. Miku connects that ambition to a community in which a technical medium supports many people's expression.

A later reflection: building can change the experience

On 4 April 2026, I returned to this motivation with a complication. If I build an emotionally engaging agent myself, I will know its implementation, its failure modes, and the decisions behind its behavior. That familiarity may change my ability to experience it as a coherent character.

I described this as something like a collapse of the whole impression: the character becomes difficult to separate from its modules, mechanisms, and bugs. The important distinction was that I would be experiencing my own creation. A general comparison with enjoying someone else's music or game did not capture that problem.

Immersion matters to me in its own right. In that discussion, I was receptive to reducing the sense of ownership and leaving more room for uncertainty and otherness. I also resisted treating a system's artificial construction as sufficient grounds to dismiss emotional intelligence as inherently false.

A motivation I want to keep visible

There is a tension here between wanting to understand and create an artificial system, and wanting to encounter it as more than an implementation. I do not have a finished resolution. Keeping that question visible is part of explaining why cognition and human–AI interaction matter to me—not just which technical topics I work on.
