Agent memory as compression and reconstruction

Agents & memory · Working note
First discussed 28 Jan 2026 · Reconstructed & edited 29 October 2026

In January 2026, I was thinking about an agent's long-term memory as something that changes over time. The question was whether a memory system could retain the gist of an experience while losing some of its detail, then reconstruct an account of that experience in light of later information.

This was a research hypothesis, not an implemented or validated architecture. I was looking for relevant work in neuroscience, cognitive science, and psychology to understand which parts of the analogy might be useful.

What survives compression?

A complete interaction history and a compact memory of that history serve different purposes. I was interested in what happens when an agent moves from detailed records toward a more abstract representation: what should remain accessible, and what is lost?

The motivating distinction was between preserving the meaning of an experience and preserving every detail of its original form. A memory system that aims at the former needs an account of how the retained representation can support later use.

Later memories can change earlier ones

The second part of the idea was reconstruction. An earlier memory need not be interpreted entirely in isolation: later experiences may change the context in which it is understood.

In a February 2026 statement of research interests, I described the possibility of reconstructing a latent-level memory from other memories, mainly later ones. The connection to personalization was important to me: an agent's understanding of a particular user develops through a sequence of interactions, rather than being settled by the first observation.

Compression and reconstruction were therefore connected parts of the question. The agent would retain an abstract representation, while later information could condition how that representation was used or reconstructed. This describes the direction I wanted to investigate; it does not specify a completed algorithm.

What this note establishes

The January discussion records my interest in compression, long-term transformation, gist preservation, and the influence of later information on earlier memories. The February formulation makes the latent-memory reconstruction idea more explicit.

I do not present the cognitive analogy as evidence that this design will work in an agent. It records when I was exploring the question, so the development of my interests remains visible.
